# Code of Conduct

<em>This text is based on the <a href="https://www.djangoproject.com/conduct/">Django Code of Conduct</a> by the Django Community, licensed under <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/">CC-BY</a>.</em>

Like the technical community as a whole, the _astro-bun_ community includes professionals and volunteers from around the world, working on every aspect of the project.

We ask every participant to follow these ground rules. They apply to maintainers, contributors, and people seeking help or guidance. This code applies to all spaces managed by the project, including the issue tracker, pull requests, and discussions.

If you believe someone is violating this code of conduct, please report it to a project maintainer through the repository's private contact channel.

- **Be friendly and patient.**
- **Be welcoming.** We welcome and support people of every background and identity, including race, ethnicity, culture, national origin, immigration status, social and economic class, education, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, age, size, family status, political belief, religion, and mental and physical ability.
- **Be considerate.** Your work affects other people and you will depend on theirs. Consider those consequences, especially when communicating across languages and time zones.
- **Be respectful.** Disagreement is no excuse for poor behavior or personal attacks. Work to resolve disagreements constructively.
- **Choose words carefully.** Harassment and exclusionary behavior are unacceptable. This includes threats, discriminatory language, sexually explicit or violent material, doxing, personal insults, unwelcome sexual attention, advocating such behavior, and repeated harassment.
- **Try to understand why we disagree.** Technical and social disagreements happen. Focus on resolving them and learning from mistakes.
